# English contents title audit

The audit is in [contents-english-title-audit.tsv](contents-english-title-audit.tsv). This is an editorial research register, not a runtime data source or TEI work-identity authority. It records every navigation entry, including section headings, prefaces and indexes, in edition order. The index is zero-based and local to the snapshot; when updating, match the literal title, contents scan and printed reference, not position alone.

## Evidence statuses

- edition-match: an external scholarly catalogue explicitly cites the Franko edition at the matching location or manuscript. Differences in printed page ranges are recorded. This is bibliographic evidence, not a new textual collation.
- title-supported: English title, collective title, saint name or genre term attested in literature. This does not establish a particular witness or recension.
- candidate: possible identification or related text; require comparison of incipit, explicit, narrative and manuscript context. Do not display as a confirmed work identity.
- editorial-container: editorial heading, front matter, index, or collection-level section. No independent work identifier is expected; individual constituent texts remain in scope.
- searched-unresolved: the search was documented but did not establish a defensible title identification; keep the literal translation and do not assign a work identifier.
- pending: no individual bibliographic verification recorded.

Current snapshot: 615 records; 25 edition matches, 152 title/terminology-supported, 76 candidates, 261 pending, 100 editorial containers, 1 searched-unresolved. All 615 translations were compared with the recorded printed-TOC transcriptions; 55 records differ from the preceding audit. This is not a new image-level collation or exhaustive bibliographic review. The `translation_review` column records that limited review scope; `catalog_refs_json` records 89 rows with 92 links (22 edition matches, 36 work-family, 34 candidate relations). Several records are editorial headings and not distinct works. No claim of exhaustive research. For a future claim of complete review, resolve each pending row with an evidence-bearing status or an explicit documented unsuccessful search; do not promote candidates by keyword overlap.

## Reader integration

In Contents, the main row opens the work as before. An adjacent book icon independently discloses references, original title, evidence, relationship, identifier and an external link. Only edition matches receive a canonical-title subtitle in the collapsed list. The filter also matches catalogue titles and identifiers. Candidate links remain labelled possible matches. OCP registry references must not be presented as available critical texts; where an OCP text is available for comparison, this still does not establish the identity of the Slavonic witness. Bibliographic labels are independent of scan/line verification. The HTML lookup is keyed by the exact volume and tuple (contents scan, literal title, printed reference); the TSV mirrors the embedded records for audit. When TEI/TF work identities become stable, promote individually substantiated relations to shared bibliography metadata, rather than generating work boundaries or merging variants from titles.

## Catalogue cross-check (2026-11-25)

Three external edition citations in e-Clavis conflict with the Franko TOC: Acts of Peter (ECCA 933; III 12–13 versus 14–15, Komarno Prologue), Ps.-Marcellus (ECCA 403; III 25–42 versus 20–25, Litmanova B), and the composite Acts of Andrew/Epistle of Achaea (ECCA 788/888; III 368–369 versus 142–145, Ossolineum 38; the cited page number exceeds the volume's final numbered page). Keep these as candidates until manuscript folios and actual text are checked. For the last item the two catalogue articles are not independent confirmation.

[Thomson Index of Slavonic Translation Literature](https://thomsonindex.org/introduction-thistl-database/) is an additional scholarly source for Slavonic translation identification. [Card 11697](https://thomsonindex.org/card/11697/) corroborates the Philip record (III 170–173, Ossolineum 38, BHG 1527). [Card 946](https://thomsonindex.org/card/946/) cites I 307–311, Zamość florilegium, but the card's work heading must be established before identification; the TOC has I 307–312. The site warns that its Transkribus card transcriptions may be uncorrected. Consult original cards, incipits, and subsequent editions before using these for confirmed work IDs.

The OCP CANON.md registry was checked for the currently linked CAVT identifiers and staged/live labels. The generic reader HTML is accessible, but the dynamic text was not independently rendered; no claim of a verified Slavonic parallel text. OCP registry entries are not a substitute for Haelewyck's original clavis.

## Volume I: Old Testament bibliography pass

The [Updated Bibliography on Jewish Pseudepigrapha in Slavonic Tradition](https://www.academia.edu/42039003/Updated_Bibliography_on_Jewish_Pseudepigrapha_in_Slavonic_Tradition) contains direct page references to Franko I. It provides exact citations for the two Adamic texts (19–23, 23–26), Lamech (37–38), and the Tale of the Blessed Zerubbabel (322), as well as collective references for the lists of books (1–6), flood (65–73), Abraham and Sarah (80–90, 330–333), Melchizedek (92–101), Isaac (102–104), Ishmael (104–106, 333–335), Joseph (124–173), David (272–279), Solomon (280–295), and the Testaments of the Twelve Patriarchs (174–224). These collective references are title-supported thematic associations, not individual work identities. The exact citation of the Zerubbabel entry warrants edition-match in the editorial audit, but no OCP registry entry was found and no runtime catalogue ID was invented. The two Adamic texts are attested as Slavonic Life of Adam and Eve; their equivalence to the Greek/Latin Life is not established, so CAVT-001 remains a comparison candidate.

The OCP registry was checked at CANON.md on master. CAVT-066 (2 Enoch), CAVT-089 (Apocalypse of Abraham), CAVT-101 (Ladder of Jacob), and CAVT-118 (Testaments of the Twelve Patriarchs) are staged, not verified live texts. CAVT-088 (Testament of Abraham) is listed as live; the public catalogue identifies TAbr.xml. The reader link points to the comparative edition, not to a verified Slavonic witness. The precise Franko citation for 2 Enoch comes from Badalanova Geller (2021), note 149, Poltava MS P. Orlov's study of the Ladder identifies Krekhiv Palaea MS F, recension A, and Franko I 108–120 in note 7. Orlov's Apocalypse of Abraham survey explicitly cites I 80–86 but warns that the Palaea conflates Abrahamic traditions: work-family only. The collective Testaments entry is an edition match; each of its twelve constituent entries is work-family. The IV Testament of Abraham is bibliographically attested in the same Updated Bibliography at IV 104–108; the Greek OCP edition is not a matched Slavonic witness.

Volume I now has 145 rows: 24 editorial containers, 4 edition matches, 61 title-supported, 7 candidates, and 49 pending. Some scholarly sources give conflicting pagination: the bibliography's Ladder entry gives I 121–122, whereas Orlov's manuscript-level citation gives I 108–120, matching the TOC. Do not apply the Ladder identifier to the separate wrestling/rods entries on 121–122. Similarly, the bibliography lists the Ascension of Isaiah at I 109–116 rather than IV 109–116; do not promote the existing IV candidate on that basis. The actual printed pages and incipits remain to be collated.

## Volume I: Pseudo-Methodius and Daniel recensions

Franko I 28 explains his editorial division of the first part of the work attributed to Methodius of Patara into thematic excerpts. Compare his notes at I 71, 76–77, 107, and 270 ([searchable OCR of the edition](https://djvu.online/file/nbMyMYV24nTfs); OCR is not a diplomatic transcription) with the [Greek recensions in English translation](https://www.pseudepigrapha.com/pseudepigrapha/ApocalypseOfPseudoMethodius_01.html) and [Syriaca.org, CPG 1830](https://syriaca.org/work/1558). Seven rows are now title-supported excerpts of the Apocalypse of Pseudo-Methodius. Their original attribution and witness readings are preserved; in particular Munt is not silently normalized to Jonetos/Moniton. The flood episode belongs to a later recension according to Franko's notes; a single Greek parallel cannot account for all the Slavonic material. CPG 1830 is recorded in the evidence only, not mislabeled as an ECCA or OCP ID.

Somov, *Traditions about the Martyrdom and Resurrection of Daniel and the Three Youths* (2021), directly discusses Franko I 307–312 and 315–319, including the possibility that the latter is an abbreviated adaptation of the Zamość text. The two are separately edition-matched in the audit; the intervening 312–315 entry remains a candidate. See also [Somov 2026](https://doi.org/10.11649/sm.3522), and his [English translations and study](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/martyrdom-of-daniel-and-the-three-youths/). The latter URL is a NASSCAL book page, not an ECCA record with a verified identifier; do not create a fictitious catalogue ID. The current OCP registry has no CAVT-267 entry.

Other reviewed items include Harut and Marut (Franko I 79 points to John VI Kantakouzenos), the Slavonic Life of Moses and Genesis of Esau, creation and Adamic traditions, the twelve stones and Job, and the Alexander/Brahman tradition. Candidates explicitly record counterevidence or ambiguity. Volume I now has 145 rows: 24 editorial containers, 6 edition matches, 69 title-supported, 16 candidates, and 30 pending. ## Public OCP destinations

User-facing links open pseudepigrapha.org, not CANON.md on GitHub. For live editions use the public reader and the XML filename listed by OCP (AdamEve.xml, TAbr.xml). For catalogue-only records use the public home/catalogue page with a standard browser text-fragment targeting the displayed CAVT identifier, for example https://pseudepigrapha.org/#:~:text=CAVT-066. This is a browser convenience, not an OCP dossier permalink: if the browser does not jump to the record, search the catalogue for the visible CAVT identifier. OCP does not expose a verified individual dossier URL in the accessible catalogue listing. Direct rendering of the reader and text-fragment navigation could not be tested in the local network environment. No change to work identity, confidence, or witness boundaries.

## Bilingual bibliography and multiple catalogues

Every user-facing work reference has `titleUk` and `evidenceUk` alongside the unchanged English title and evidence. Catalogue labels and confidence labels switch with the UI locale; the canonical subtitle and expanded notes switch without collapsing the disclosure. Search indexes both languages and all catalogue IDs. Original source headings, names of publications in citations, manuscript shelfmarks and numerical identifiers are preserved. The same entry can carry more than one provider: for the provisional Vision of Isaiah (IV), ECCA 983 explicitly cross-lists CAVT 218, and OCP's registry lists Ascensio Isaiae as staged. Both links are displayed; neither confirms the precise Slavonic text. Catalogue-only links retain the public search fallback. All other existing relations are unchanged. The TSV's JSON column includes both translations; the editorial audit columns remain in English.

## Volume I: Palaea excerpts and prophet lives (follow-up to #210)

Reviewed 16 formerly pending headings against the provisional full-page readings and Franko's own notes. The TSV points to the specific pilot JSON containing each source, so the evidence can be reproduced and later checked against the scan. These are not independent human or image collations. Fourteen pending headings remain in volume I. Historical counts above describe their respective passes.

* The Dubivtsi creation text and the two Paradise witnesses are now described by content and manuscript rather than by treating a witness heading as a unique work. Franko I 18 distinguishes the Palaea account from the Bilakhevych text, whose extra details concern a lapidary.
* For Cain, Seth, Enoch, Babel, Serug/Nahor and Jacob's rods and wrestling, the printed commentary cites exact pages/columns of the Tolkovaya Palaea. The Adamic genealogy also cites Porfiriev's parallel from George Hamartolos. The Enoch genealogy and the Jacob material following the Ladder are not automatically CAVT-066 or CAVT-101. Franko explicitly states that the Esau/Jacob birth text mainly retells Genesis 25:21–27 and omits a large geography section in the Babel excerpt.
* I 30, the Krekhiv Palaea excerpt On the making of the Holy Trinity, is a narrative of Seth seeking the oil of mercy. Its sequence of prophecy, 5500 years, resurrection and baptism closely parallels [Latin Descent into Hell A, ch. 3](https://ccel.org/ccel/anonymous/apocrypha_nt/anf08.vii.xvi.iii.html) and [Latin Life of Adam and Eve 41–42](https://www.marquette.edu/maqom/latinlifeadameve.html). The burial ending differs; do not identify the entire excerpt with either complete work, or infer the direction of borrowing from the Latin witnesses. Both related catalogues are provided: ECCA 309 at work-family level and CAVT-001 as a candidate parallel. The distinct Western Legend of the Holy Rood Tree (ECCA 121) also shares the oil motif but is not assigned on that basis. Franko himself cites Tikhonravov I 17–18 and the Novgorod Palaea of 1477, absent in the other two collated Palaea witnesses.
* Franko I 303 and 304 explicitly trace the Elijah birth dream and Prologue prophet lives to the text attributed to Epiphanius of Cyprus (Historia de prophetarum vita et interitu). The I 296–303 Elijah narrative also retells Kings, whereas the I 303–304 Elisha text is an epitome. OCP CAVT-213 (Vitae prophetarum) is linked at work-family level for both. I 304–305 Isaiah has a comparable opening, but only its first page was available in the pilot; CAVT-213 remains a candidate for it. The [public OCP catalogue](https://pseudepigrapha.org/#:~:text=CAVT-213) marks the edition in progress; no verified ECCA record for Lives of the Prophets was found. Do not substitute Ascension of Isaiah or Apocalypse of Elijah solely because of the prophet's name.

All five new user-facing links are bilingual, with exact source citations and the limitations of the association retained. No English navigation title or printed source title was changed; the descriptions in the audit are scholarly working labels.
