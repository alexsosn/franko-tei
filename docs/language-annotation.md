# Contextual language attribution (working draft)
 
## Research and editorial decision

TEI P5 [language](https://vault.tei-c.de/P5/current/doc/tei-p5-doc/en/html/ref-language.html) uses BCP 47 identifiers, see [RFC 5646](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/rfc5646/). Unicode [UAX 24](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr24/) distinguishes script properties from language, including Common and Inherited characters. Universal Dependencies uses [cu](https://universaldependencies.org/treebanks/cu_proiel/index.html) for Old Church Slavonic and [orv](https://universaldependencies.org/treebanks/orv_ruthenian/index.html) for its Ruthenian historical corpus; the latter is a broad Old East Slavic code, not a dedicated historical Ukrainian identifier. Do not confuse historical Ruthenian with modern Rusyn (rue). These precedents support interoperability, not automatic classification of a Franko witness.

## Annotation contract

Use the existing line-level language_spans, with half-open Unicode code-point offsets into the exact named reading, not UTF-16 indices or normalized text. Preserve combining marks/titla with their bases. Distinguish language (BCP 47) and script (ISO 15924). For new annotations prefer cu for Church Slavonic (legacy chu remains readable), uk for Franko's Ukrainian, ru for Russian, grc for ancient Greek, la for Latin, de/fr/pl/hr as applicable. Use orv provisionally for contextually supported historical Ruthenian (uncertain=true), und for unresolved language, zxx for nonlinguistic material such as standalone folio numbers. Assign punctuation and spacing to adjacent contextual spans; Roman numerals in prose are not evidence of Latin language. Do not infer language from script alone. Greek, Latin, or Cyrillic characters anomalous within a frozen word may be marked und with their observed Unicode script rather than corrected silently.

Annotate complete lexical coverage in new drafts, including within-line citations, source variants, footnote text and short quotations. Punctuation and digits may travel with their contextual span. If exact linguistic classification is unresolved, record und and uncertainty rather than omitting the range. Language certainty is distinct from reading certainty. All pages remain agent_unverified. Historic recensions, register, and orthographic tradition require evidence from witness identity and sustained linguistic analysis; do not infer them from isolated graphemes. Defer adding distinct TEI/TF features for them until representative examples and requirements are established.

## Preserve versions and parallel work

Never change frozen line text, raw response, reader ID, geometry, source links, exposure history or uncertainty notes during a language-only pass. Update language_spans on the same existing candidate in main; record a separate top-level language_annotation with source record commit/blob, annotation model and method. Earlier annotations remain recoverable from Git. The annotation is not a fresh blind transcription. New candidates on the contents-navigation or transcription branches should be annotated during creation by their owning agent; do not edit their files concurrently. Check changed filenames before creating an annotation PR; if overlapping changes appear, reconcile after merge by reader ID and exact literal offsets, never transplant annotations from a different reading. Existing contents anchors (candidate/reader/line/literal) remain valid.

## Printed apparatus labels

Printed editorial footnote numbers and paired apparatus delimiters (e.g. `¹)`, `¹⁰—`, `⁷—⁷)`) are nonlinguistic: mark the complete label `zxx` / `Zyyy` (Common script), retaining exact code-point offsets. Preserve historical Cyrillic numerals with titla in their contextual language; ordinary Arabic digits, ambiguous superscripts and punctuation need contextual review. Do not infer note links from this typography. Run `python -m franko_tei.footnote_audit` for a read-only JSONL inventory of candidate labels overlapping `cu` or `chu` ranges across discovered readings. The audit is intentionally not a complete footnote detector; inspect ASCII labels and ambiguous formats separately. See #281.
